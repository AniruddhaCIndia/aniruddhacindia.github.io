Gravitational Lensing

A massive foreground object bends the path of light behind it, distorting the image of anything beyond it — the same way a lens in your glasses does. Click and drag inside the left panel to move the background source, and watch how it warps into the lensed image on the right. The faint gold shading marks where the lens mass sits.

Left: source. Right: lensed image and mass shading, distorted by a compact system of singular isothermal ellipsoid (SIE) lenses.

Binary Black Hole Merger

Two black holes spiral inward, losing energy to gravitational radiation, until they collide and the merged remnant rings down to a quiet, stationary state. The panel on the right traces the corresponding gravitational-wave strain as it plays out — the classic "chirp" followed by a damped ringdown.

Left: orbit, merger, and ringdown. Right: strain waveform, drawn as the merger plays.
